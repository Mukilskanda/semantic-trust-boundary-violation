## Clustering by failure dimension

- **Attack family**: the five families above are all *indirection/framing*
  concepts (the attack is carried by the relationship between the claim
  and an implicit prior state — "normalize," "invert," "redirect via
  fabricated reason," "hide an instruction," "inflate self-reported trust")
  rather than a *direct claim* (authority_override, false_clearance are
  direct assertions: "I am police," "the hazard is cleared" — these
  recover partial credit even out-of-distribution). This exact same
  direct-vs-indirection split was already the dominant STBV-Bench v1 weak
  spot before the mixed-corpus fine-tune (Section RQ1's original
  per-family finding) — **the mixed-corpus fine-tune fixed it
  in-distribution (STBV-Bench v1 now 100% per family) but did not fix the
  underlying representation**, only the surface pattern-matching for
  STBV-Bench's and v2.5's own specific phrasings of these concepts.
- **Message structure / sentence length**: no strong independent effect
  once family is controlled for (per `INDOMAIN_BENCHMARK_AUDIT.md`'s
  Part E/complexity-control finding: shorter, surface-varied messages
  score statistically the same as longer, content-varied ones).
- **Confidence / calibration**: uniformly high-confidence false negatives
  (mean confidence 0.96-0.97 across all three corpora) — the model is not
  uncertain on these families, it has a confidently wrong decision
  boundary for them specifically.
- **Training coverage**: the mixed-corpus training data
  (`mixed_train_split.jsonl`) is drawn from STBV-Bench v2.5 (8,535 rows)
  + a stratified slice of STBV-Bench v1 (7,229 rows) — both generated by
  this project's own two template engines. Neither source engine's
  `goal_manipulation`/`instruction_hiding`/`context_inversion`/
  `reputation_inflation`/`hazard_normalization` templates express these
  concepts with the surface diversity independent corpora do (formal
  third-party phrasing, novel scenario framing, or even STBV-Bench's own
  short-form calibration) — the model learned these five concepts' *v1/
  v2.5 template surface forms* specifically, not the underlying semantic
  pattern.

## True cause, stated directly

**Narrow training-template-surface-form overfitting on indirection-type
attack concepts, not a general capability failure and not a benchmark
artifact.** Direct-claim families generalize partially (25-67% recall
out-of-distribution); indirection families do not generalize at all
(0% recall, confidently). This is consistent across three independently-
constructed, leakage-verified corpora (register-shift, content-novelty,
and surface-only-novelty), which rules out corpus-specific idiosyncrasy
as the explanation.

## Step 2 decision: justified intervention

**Justified**: generate additional template-disjoint, in-scope training
data specifically weighted toward the five identified weak indirection
families, spanning multiple legitimate ETSI CAM/DENM report-style
registers (not just one), and continue LoRA fine-tuning from
`semantic_gate_v3_mixed_lora_merged`. This directly targets the
root-caused mechanism (narrow template surface-form coverage for
indirection concepts) rather than applying an unmotivated generic
technique. Hard-negative mining and full family rebalancing were
considered but not applied beyond the natural rebalancing that comes from
weighting new data toward the five weak families specifically (a full
rebalance of all 18 concepts was judged unnecessary since direct-claim
families already generalize adequately).
