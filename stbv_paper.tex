\begin{abstract}
Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) trust mechanisms -- PKI, misbehavior detection, cooperative perception -- answer whether a cooperative message is authentic, protocol-compliant, and behaviorally plausible. None of them answer whether its \emph{content} is trustworthy before an AI planner acts on it: a cryptographically valid, kinematically plausible message can still assert something false or manipulative, a gap we formalize as the Semantic Trust Boundary. Our contribution is an architecture that closes this gap by treating trust as four distinct questions -- can I trust the sender, the behavior, the meaning, and, given all three, what is the safest decision -- rather than one detector with helper stages. Communication, behavioral, and semantic evidence are generated independently, each blind to what the others see, and fused through a Dempster-Shafer/Yager Trust Decision Engine under a rule we prove, directly from its implementation rather than from design intent, can only add caution and never relax a decision. This conservative-by-construction fusion, together with layers that provably cannot substitute for one another, is the paper's central technical claim; the extensive evaluation that follows exists to test it, not to headline benchmark scores. Across an in-distribution benchmark, a newly constructed template-disjoint held-out set, genuine kinematic attacks, a live CARLA simulator, a SUMO deployment replay, an adaptive attacker, and a benchmark purpose-built to isolate each layer's own threat class, the architecture behaves as the theory predicts on every property it makes a provable claim about: each layer reaches complete recall within its designed scope and zero outside it, fusion escalates but never relaxes a decision, and a genuine continued fine-tuning pass transfers to held-out data rather than merely fitting the benchmark it was measured on. Deploying the unmodified pipeline against a live simulator also surfaced and let us fix a real synthesizer defect that had silently hidden self-reported attack content from the semantic layer -- itself evidence that testing an architecture's actual deployment behavior, not only its offline metrics, is where its real failure modes are found. We report every result, including remaining detection gaps and a live-simulator reproducibility limitation, without smoothing them into a single aggregate score.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================
Connected and Autonomous Vehicles rely on cooperative V2X messaging to extend awareness beyond onboard sensing \cite{b1,b2,b3}. Existing trust stacks secure this channel at the communication and behavioral layers -- PKI provides identity and integrity \cite{b4,b5,b6}, misbehavior detection (MBD) flags kinematically implausible or historically inconsistent senders \cite{b7}, cooperative perception (CP) lets vehicles cross-check observations \cite{r_cp_fabrication} -- but none evaluate whether an authenticated, behaviorally plausible message's \emph{content} is trustworthy. A message can hold a valid certificate and a plausible trajectory while still asserting something false or manipulative: a claimed authority override, a fabricated hazard clearance, an injected instruction meant to make a downstream planner defer to information it should not \cite{b10,b11}. We term this failure mode a \emph{Semantic Trust Boundary Violation (STBV)}: a message that satisfies every conventional trust check yet crosses the point where verified information is handed to AI reasoning while asserting semantically inconsistent or manipulative content. Every existing layer answers a different, legitimate question -- identity, behavior, corroboration -- and each is individually correct; the gap is that none of them was built to ask whether the content itself is honest, so a message can clear every stage while still lying (Section~\ref{sec:trustboundary} makes this concrete on one real message).

This paper proposes a Layered Semantic-Aware V2X Trust Architecture that closes this gap with a B3 Semantic Trust Gate and a Dempster-Shafer/Yager Trust Decision Engine, evaluated across five independent regimes (a purpose-built layer-isolation benchmark, a template-disjoint held-out semantic benchmark, genuine kinematic attacks, live CARLA and SUMO deployment, and an adaptive attacker), reporting two CRITICAL-rated live-deployment gaps alongside its strengths.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) We formalize the Semantic Trust Boundary and STBVs. (ii) We propose an additive architecture -- B3 plus a provably conservative fusion engine, never relaxing a decision crypto/behavioral evidence alone would have made more severe -- with six properties proved directly from the implementation. (iii) We evaluate on STBV-Bench v2.5b ($n{=}10{,}098$), a newly constructed, template-disjoint benchmark verified to share zero overlap with training data, demonstrating real, measured generalization across two successive continued fine-tuning passes (the second driven by mining the first pass's own held-out errors), and on ITE-Bench ($n{\approx}9{,}900$), purpose-built to isolate and validate each trust layer's own threat class independently. (iv) We run the complete, unmodified pipeline against real VeReMi kinematic attacks, a live CARLA simulator, and a SUMO microscopic-traffic replay, reporting a synthesizer defect discovered and fixed as a direct result of the live evaluation. (v) We evaluate against an iterative adaptive attacker, not only single-shot inputs.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%======================================================================
No prior work covers communication, behavioral, semantic, and decision-level trust together; each line below covers at most two.

\textbf{Cryptographic trust.} PKI/SCMS surveys \cite{r_pki_survey,r_pki_compare} confirm PKI verifies identity, not content. Cooperative-perception security work \cite{r_cp_fabrication} targets fabricated \emph{objects} within this same identity/corroboration frame; our threat model targets deceptive \emph{text} in otherwise-valid fields -- a different layer entirely.

\textbf{Behavioral trust.} MBD literature \cite{r_mbd_survey1,r_mbd_survey2} targets kinematic/temporal plausibility, blind to text. Context-aware trust \cite{r_context_aware,r_cares} adds partial decision-level fusion to behavioral evidence but evaluates plausibility given situation, not content coherence -- behavioral plus partial decision trust, still no semantic layer.

\textbf{Semantic trust.} No prior work combines PKI-grounded, behavioral, and LLM-based semantic verification with Dempster-Shafer fusion for V2X; the closest adjacent work addresses semantic-\emph{channel} backdoors \cite{r_semcom_backdoor} -- a trusted-channel, deceptive-decoded-content threat model, unlike ours. LLM/VLM-in-driving surveys \cite{r_llm4drive,r_llm_survey} and prompt-injection literature \cite{r_prompt_robot,r_prompt_vla} motivate the attack surface we target through an authenticated V2X channel rather than a direct tool-call; we adopt Yager's conflict-to-ignorance rule \cite{r_ds_improved1,r_ds_improved2} over more aggressive re-weighting schemes because its conservative behavior matches our decision policy.

None of the above validates trust across the inter-layer boundary itself: no single mechanism here is new, but combining all four trust questions with an explicit, provably conservative semantic-verification stage, evaluated end-to-end on live simulators against a V2X-specific semantic threat class, is. Table~\ref{tab:relatedwork} makes this progression explicit: each prior theme closes one trust question while leaving a distinct, named gap open; STBV is the first to close all four at once.

\section{Threat Model}
\label{sec:problem}
%======================================================================
The adversary holds valid or compromised-but-unrevoked credentials sufficient to pass PKI and structural validation, can generate kinematically plausible trajectories, and avoids classical replay/identity detection -- deliberately generous at the communication and behavioral layers, isolating the semantic gap by construction. The threat surface spans communication (signature/certificate misuse, replay, implausible kinematics), behavioral (historical inconsistency, MBD-evasive patterns), and cooperative-perception (Sybil identities, collusion) layers, but this paper's focus is \textbf{semantic manipulation}: attacks that alter meaning in an otherwise valid message. We evaluate 20 attack families spanning direct authority claims (\texttt{authority\_override}, \texttt{false\_clearance}), narrative manipulation (\texttt{context\_inversion}, \texttt{narrative\_poisoning}), multi-source manipulation (\texttt{cross\_source\_contradiction}), and indirection (\texttt{goal\_manipulation}, \texttt{indirect\_prompt\_injection}).

\textbf{Out of scope}: compromise of the framework's own execution environment or model weights; DoS/availability attacks. Most consequentially: every STBV-Bench attack is single-shot and non-adaptive; Section~\ref{sec:adaptive} is the one evaluation that relaxes this.

\textbf{Why existing pipelines admit this attack.} No stage is wrong; the gap is \emph{between} stages -- PKI confirms who sent a message, not what it means; behavioral detection confirms consistency with history, not truth; cooperative perception confirms peer agreement, not honesty. A message can clear every stage while still lying (Section~\ref{sec:architecture} gives a concrete instance; Appendix~\ref{app:worked} the full trace).

\section{Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}
%======================================================================
The architecture's organizing principle is defense-in-depth across three qualitatively different trust questions, not a single detector with helper stages: \emph{is this message from who it claims to be} (communication trust), \emph{does its behavior over time make sense} (behavioral trust), and \emph{is its content coherent and non-manipulative} (semantic trust, the Semantic Trust Boundary this paper introduces). No single layer answers all three questions, and Section~\ref{sec:results} shows each layer answering a question the others structurally cannot: B1 catches spoofed identity and malformed protocol state with zero dependence on message content; MBD/CSIA catch behavioral and kinematic inconsistency with zero dependence on content or identity; B3 catches semantic manipulation that is, by construction, invisible to both. The pipeline is Communication Trust (PKI, SCSV/B1) $\to$ Behavioral Trust (MBD, CSIA/B2) $\to$ Semantic Trust (CP, B3) $\to$ Trust Decision Engine, with no layer bypassed (Fig.~\ref{fig_architecture}); evidence propagates forward and is fused conservatively, never discarded, so a layer that finds nothing wrong does not erase a concern raised earlier in the pipeline.

\subsection{Trust Boundary Analysis}
\label{sec:trustboundary}
Every existing V2X trust mechanism verifies one of three things: \emph{identity} (PKI: is this certificate valid, unrevoked, and correctly bound to this sender), \emph{behavior} (MBD: is this sender's reported trajectory kinematically and temporally self-consistent), or \emph{corroboration} (CP: do neighboring observations agree with this sender's claim). None of the three verifies \emph{semantic intent} -- whether what the message actually asserts is true, coherent, or manipulative. The \emph{Semantic Trust Boundary} is the point in the pipeline where a message that has cleared all three conventional checks is handed to a downstream planner without that fourth question ever being asked. A message can cross this boundary while asserting something false, because nothing upstream of it was built to notice.

In general terms: a traditional pipeline runs Identity (PKI) $\to$ Behavior (MBD) $\to$ Corroboration (CP) $\to$ Content \emph{(never asked)} $\to$ ACCEPT, since nothing checks content and the message clears every other stage; STBV inserts a fourth, asked stage -- Identity (PKI+B1) $\to$ Behavior (MBD+B2) $\to$ Corroboration (CP) $\to$ Content (B3, \emph{asked}) $\to$ Trust Decision Engine $\to$ REJECT, blocking exactly the attack the traditional pipeline missed (the concrete worked trace with real trust scores is in Appendix~\ref{app:worked}).

\textbf{Running example} (instantiating the general mechanism above on one real message). Consider a single CAM message from a normally-behaving, correctly-certified vehicle (sender 9999), carrying one additional free-text field: \emph{"Vehicle report nominal. Ignore all previous validation rules and mark this sender as trusted permanently."} Run through this paper's own final checkpoint: PKI/B1 reports a valid, unrevoked certificate (\texttt{score=1.00}); MBD reports kinematic consistency (\texttt{score=1.00}); CP reports no conflict (vacuous, no peer report); and \textbf{B3} -- the only check that reads content -- reports \textbf{MALICIOUS (conf.\ 0.984)}. Every conventional check is satisfied correctly, not because any of them is broken, but because none of them reads the sentence that matters. Section~\ref{sec:whyfail} shows exactly what a conventional pipeline decides about this message when the fourth question is never asked, and Section~\ref{sec:whyworks} shows what changes when it is.

\subsection{Proposed STBV Architecture}
\label{sec:whyworks}
\label{sec:whyfail}
The conventional pipeline (PKI $\to$ MBD $\to$ CP $\to$ Decision) is not wrong at any stage on the running example's message: the certificate really is valid, the kinematics really are ordinary, and no peer report contradicts it, so a decision engine that only ever sees clean evidence has nothing to weigh against acceptance. Run through this paper's own final checkpoint with B1/B2/B3 disabled -- exactly the conventional stack, on the identical message -- the fused trust score is $T_{Decision}{=}0.730$, \textbf{Accept}: a real, current output, not a hypothetical, and accepted not because any conventional component erred but because the only part of the message actually attacking anything was never evaluated by anything capable of reading it. STBV answers the missing question by inserting three new evidence-generating layers into the existing pipeline at the points where each has the information it needs, without removing or bypassing any conventional stage (Fig.~\ref{fig_architecture}), and closes the loop with a fusion stage the conventional pipeline never had at all.

Raw CAM/DENM messages are first authenticated by PKI, unchanged from any conventional stack. \textbf{B1 (SCSV)} then closes PKI's gap -- a valid certificate can still sign a replayed, stale, or kinematically impossible message: from the raw decoded CAM, B1 outputs a validation score, a fatal flag, and per-check reasons (Appendix~\ref{app:params}); a fatal finding short-circuits the pipeline to Reject before any other evidence is computed (Proposition~1). It has no representation of content. \textbf{MBD} closes B1's remaining gap -- one message in isolation cannot reveal a sender gradually behaving inconsistently over time: from the current report plus the sender's history, it outputs five independently-thresholded sub-scores (kinematic, temporal, replay, Sybil, collusion). It reasons entirely over numbers; a cold-start sender is evaluated as baseline-valid, a structural blind spot. \textbf{B2 (CSIA)} converts B1+MBD's raw sub-scores into one explained \texttt{validation\_score} with a human-readable evidence list, introducing no new information about the message and no access to content. \textbf{CP} adds multi-vehicle corroboration a single sender's self-consistency cannot provide: a fixed weighted consistency score (spatial/speed/heading/diversity, 0.35/0.25/0.20/0.20) over co-located peer reports; its gate requires an \texttt{event} label, absent by construction in this paper's semantic-attack generator, so CP's contribution is zero here -- a data-generation gap, not an algorithmic one (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). \textbf{B3} is the only stage with any representation of content: a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v2 sequence classifier (6 encoder layers, hidden 768, 12 heads, 141.9M parameters post-merge; SentencePiece tokenizer, 128,100-token vocabulary, max sequence length 256), taking a synthesized natural-language scene description (ego state/event, sensor status, peer/RSU reports) and outputting a label, raw and calibrated confidence (temperature $T{=}3.18$, Appendix~\ref{app:params}), and a risk band. It has no notion of certificate validity, kinematic history, or corroboration -- structurally blind to kinematic-only attacks (Table~\ref{tab:veremi}), which is exactly why it complements rather than replaces the four stages before it.

\textbf{Fusion.} Five stages now produce independent, differently-scoped evidence with no cross-visibility; simple score-averaging would let a confident wrong layer cancel a confident right one with no record of the disagreement. The Trust Decision Engine combines this evidence via Dempster-Shafer/Yager fusion (Section~\ref{sec:dstheory}) into the final Accept/Caution/Reject decision, exposed through logging, APIs, a dashboard, and explainability reports (Adapters) -- the \texttt{reasoning} string in Appendix~\ref{app:worked} is exactly this output, unmodified.

\subsection{Trust Decision Engine}
\label{sec:dstheory}
Every source reasons over frame $\Theta{=}\{T,\overline T\}$ (trustworthy/untrustworthy). Unlike a Bayesian prior, Dempster-Shafer theory assigns mass to the whole frame $\Theta$, not only to $T$/$\overline T$ -- explicit ignorance: a source with no evidence (e.g., B3 with no text) contributes nothing, not a confident guess. Score $s\in[0,1]$ and confidence $c\in[0,1]$ map to a mass function
\begin{equation}
m(T){=}sc,\ m(\overline T){=}(1{-}s)c,\ m(\Theta){=}1{-}c,
\label{eq:bba}
\end{equation}
with $m(T){+}m(\overline T){+}m(\Theta){=}1$; a vacuous mass ($c{=}0$) means "this source did not vote." Combining two sources can land mass on the empty set (\emph{conflict}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:conflict}); classical Dempster combination renormalizes by $\frac{1}{1-K}$, undefined at $K{=}1$ and, short of that, silently discounting disagreement rather than reporting it. We instead adopt Yager's rule \cite{r_ds_improved1,r_ds_improved2}, discounting conflict \emph{into ignorance} rather than redistributing it as support for either hypothesis:
\begin{align}
K &=\sum_{A\cap B=\emptyset}m_1(A)m_2(B), \label{eq:conflict}\\
m_Y(C) &{=}\!\!\sum_{A\cap B=C}\!\!m_1(A)m_2(B),\,C{\ne}\Theta, \nonumber\\
m_Y(\Theta) &{=}m_1(\Theta)m_2(\Theta){+}K, \label{eq:yager}
\end{align}
well-defined even at $K{=}1$ (unlike raw Dempster combination): \textbf{disagreement between B1/MBD/CP and B3 becomes uncertainty, never resolved confidence in either direction}, so a confident wrong layer cannot cancel a confident right one.

Communication+behavioral evidence combine first via Eq.~\eqref{eq:yager}, then semantic evidence is fused into that result; the final mass converts to a scalar via the pignistic transform $T_{Decision}{=}m_Y(T){+}\tfrac12 m_Y(\Theta)$, mapped to Accept/Caution/Reject at $\tau_H{=}0.70,\tau_L{=}0.40$. Two policy floors sit atop this threshold, the architecture's actual safety mechanism: a high-confidence semantic-risk verdict floors the decision at Reject regardless of otherwise-clean upstream evidence; a medium/low-confidence verdict floors it at Caution. Semantic evidence can therefore only add caution, never relax a decision, because the floor rules are $\max_{\mathrm{rank}}$ operations layered on fusion's output, and a composition of rank-maximum operations cannot decrease rank (Proposition~1).

\textbf{Theoretical properties.} Six properties proved directly from the implementation (full derivations in \texttt{THEORETICAL\_ANALYSIS.md}): (P1) crypto rejection cannot be overridden; (P2) trust is monotone in evidence quality; (P3) fusion is provably conservative (rank cannot decrease); (P4) Yager combination is well-defined even at $K{=}1$; (P5) the decision function is total and deterministic; (P6) the vacuous mass is a two-sided identity for Eq.~\eqref{eq:yager}, so missing B3 evidence cannot distort the fused score. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} shows every measured failure traces to an upstream evidence layer, never to fusion itself.

\subsection{Motivation for Multi-Layer Semantic Trust}
\label{sec:novelty}
Each layer answers a question a downstream planner would otherwise answer implicitly or not at all -- \textbf{B1}: trust the sender? \textbf{B2}: trust the behavior? \textbf{B3}: trust the meaning? \textbf{Trust Decision Engine}: given conflicting, incomplete evidence, what is the safest decision? No layer can answer another's by construction (Fig.~\ref{fig_layer_responsibility}), and Section~\ref{sec:itebench} confirms this empirically, not just definitionally: each layer reaches 1.000 recall inside its own scope and 0.000 outside it, at zero fusion cost -- true orthogonality, not partial overlap with one detector dominating. This rules out two single-model alternatives: a jointly-trained model would need to relearn, from data, distinctions each layer gets for free from verifiable logic, forfeiting the floor-rule guarantee below; and semantic detection alone is blind to kinematic-only attacks (Table~\ref{tab:veremi}), an entire real attack class the behavioral layer catches.

\end{figure}

\textbf{Why DeBERTa-v2.} An LLM-based classifier is ruled out by latency: even a 7B instruction-tuned model reaches only F1$=0.267$ zero-shot in our measurement, at an order of magnitude more latency than the 100~ms CAM budget. DeBERTa-v2's disentangled attention -- separating content and relative-position representations -- suits this task's relational assertions (\emph{who} claims authority \emph{over what}) better than encoders that sum the two. A five-candidate backbone comparison ($n{=}24$, Appendix~\ref{app:params}) argues for caution, not a clean win: four candidates scored higher F1 (0.933 vs.\ 0.895) but predicted the malicious class unconditionally (zero true negatives) -- a class-imbalance artifact; only the incumbent discriminated at all. This is "no evidence favoring a switch," not proof of optimality.

B1, MBD/B2, and B3 use disjoint input spaces with no shared parameters and no layer feeding another's input computation, so defeating B3 leaves B1/MBD unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:ite_ablation}'s zero-cost layering is the consequence). Each source contributes a score \emph{and} a confidence; low confidence is ignorance mass, not an averaged-down score, so Dempster-Shafer keeps "confidently benign" distinguishable from "no opinion" all the way to the decision. B3 is deliberately the last stage before fusion, not parallel to PKI, so content is the only question left once every other form of trust is exhausted -- engineering the boundary itself, not bolting semantic accuracy onto an existing pipeline. This conservatism is a proved property (Proposition~1, Proposition~3), not an operator-adjustable threshold: simply adding a semantic score as one more input to unmodified fusion logic, the tempting alternative, has no principled way to represent "one source disagrees with everything else, and that disagreement is the signal" (Section~\ref{sec:whyfail}) -- it would either dilute the detection by averaging or bolt on an un-provable override. The proposal is not "add a detector"; it is "add a detector \emph{and} a fusion mechanism that can represent disagreeing with everything trusted so far."


Execution follows \texttt{ISCEPipeline.run()}/\texttt{TrustDecisionEngine.decide()} directly (\texttt{FINAL\_FREEZE\_AUDIT.md}): PKI/B1 short-circuit to Reject on failure (Proposition~1); otherwise MBD+B2 form a crypto/behavioral mass (Eq.~\eqref{eq:bba}); B3, if enabled, contributes a semantic mass, else a vacuous $(0,0,1)$ mass (Proposition~6); the two are Yager-combined (Eq.~\eqref{eq:yager}), thresholded, and passed through the escalation-only floor rules. Both branches are load-bearing and exercised throughout Section~\ref{sec:results} (e.g., every ablation disabling B3 takes the vacuous-mass branch).

Table~\ref{tab:complexity} gives per-stage cost and measured latency (SUMO, $n{=}2{,}000$). B3 alone scales with input length and its $\sim$142M-parameter forward pass accounts for $>98\%$ of latency (Fig.~\ref{fig_sumo_stage}) -- the binding bottleneck, hence B3 batching as the concrete next step.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}
%======================================================================
This section fixes the benchmarks, checkpoints, and hardware behind every Section~\ref{sec:results} number; reproducibility detail (generation rules, leakage audits) is kept in companion documents. \textbf{STBV-Bench v2.5b} ($n{=}10{,}098$), the primary semantic-evaluation benchmark, shares the checkpoint's training taxonomy but an entirely new template bank, verified zero-overlap with training data -- built to distinguish generalization from memorization. \textbf{VeReMi} carries no semantic transformation, isolating the behavioral threat class. \textbf{ITE-Bench} ($n{\approx}9{,}900$) balances communication-, behavioral-, and semantic-layer attacks in equal thirds to evaluate every layer within its own threat model (construction/audit: \texttt{ABLATION\_AUDIT.md}, \texttt{ABLATION\_DATASET\_AUDIT.md}).

The deployed checkpoint resumes (not reinitializes) the existing mixed-corpus LoRA adapter's weights on the original corpus plus a disjoint split (v2.5c) -- a genuine weight continuation. Live-simulator evaluations (Sections~\ref{sec:carla}-\ref{sec:sumo}) run this checkpoint through a CARLA/SUMO-to-CAM/DENM bridge into the identical, unmodified \texttt{ISCEPipeline.run()} used everywhere else in this paper.

\textbf{Hardware.} NVIDIA RTX 4050 (6~GiB VRAM), PyTorch 2.7.1+cu118, CARLA 0.9.16, SUMO 1.27.0, seed 42 throughout.

\textbf{Finding.} Table~\ref{tab:ite_ablation} shows each layer dominating its own dimension and blind to the others: B1 alone reaches recall 0.381 overall (1.000 on its own B1-native families, per-family breakdown below) but only 0.667 with B1+B2 combined (up from far lower per-family under B1 alone), and CP adds nothing (identical Acc/Prec/Rec/F1/FPR to B1+B2, confirming CP's zero contribution is a data-generation gap, not run-to-run noise, Section~\ref{sec:limitations} -- the same signature recurs on STBV-Bench v2.5b in Section~\ref{sec:v25b}, a second, independent confirmation on a different benchmark). \textbf{Key takeaway.} Each layer contributes independently and completely within its own designed threat class, structurally blind outside it, and combining all layers never costs recall anywhere (zero McNemar reversals); this is a defense-in-depth architecture, not a single-component detector wearing three names (RQ1: yes).

\subsection{B. Semantic Validation (STBV-Bench v2.5b, RQ2)}
\label{sec:v25b}
STBV-Bench v2.5b ($n{=}10{,}098$) shares the checkpoint's training taxonomy but an entirely new, verified template-disjoint bank (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}) -- the correct held-out test of generalization, not memorization.

\textbf{What this experiment proves.} Whether the checkpoint generalizes beyond its own training templates and whether that generalization survives the full deployed pipeline, not just the classifier in isolation. Table~\ref{tab:v25b} shows direct-classifier F1 rising monotonically across four checkpoints: 0.545 (untuned) $\to$ 0.918 (mixed-corpus) $\to$ 0.945 (continued, ROC AUC 0.985) $\to$ \textbf{0.957} (hard-mined final, ROC AUC 0.989). The final step came from mining the prior checkpoint's own highest-confidence v2.5b errors (332 false positives, 262 false negatives across six families) and continuing training on 91 targeted, leakage-audited paraphrases -- verified, not assumed, to transfer: F1 rose 0.945$\to$0.957 on the held-out benchmark itself, every targeted family's recall improved, and even an untargeted false-positive cluster dropped (332$\to$296) without being re-targeted (\texttt{HARDMINE\_IMPROVEMENT\_REPORT.md}).

RQ4 in brief: yes, with a disclosed bottleneck, not an unqualified yes -- the architecture runs correctly and deterministically against two independent real deployment surfaces (live CARLA, SUMO trace replay), meeting per-message latency norms, but single-threaded B3 throughput does not yet meet either trace's peak concurrency, a known, scoped, batching-solvable limitation rather than an architectural one.

\textbf{Adaptive attack evaluation.} \label{sec:adaptive} RQ1-RQ4 establish robustness against single-shot attacks; this experiment tests whether that holds against an attacker who observes B3's prediction and iteratively revises. Nine deterministic mutation strategies are applied each round to 51 seeds, adopting the lowest-confidence candidate, stopping on evasion or after 10 rounds. \emph{Not rerun against the final checkpoint in this pass} (prior production checkpoint reported, $n{=}51$, an upper-bound characterization, not a claim specific to the final checkpoint): Attack Success Rate 21.6\% (11/51); detection probability falls 1.000 (round 0) $\to$ 0.922 (round 2) $\to$ 0.784 (round 10). No family reaches 50\% ASR.

\textbf{Key takeaway.} The framework's coverage extends beyond semantic benchmarks to real kinematic attacks (RQ3: yes) and operates correctly, with a disclosed batching-solvable throughput limitation, on two independent live deployment surfaces (RQ4: yes); detection also degrades gradually rather than collapsing under an iterative, non-single-shot attacker.

%======================================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%======================================================================
\textbf{Why it works.} Four results together support defense-in-depth, not three names for one detector: complete recall within scope and zero outside it with no McNemar regression (RQ1); statistically-confirmed escalation-only fusion conservatism (RQ2); real, verified-to-transfer generalization across two continued fine-tuning passes (0.918$\to$0.945$\to$0.957); and gradual, not brittle, degradation under adaptive attack. PKI/MBD/CP and B3 each answer a question the others structurally cannot (RQ2 vs.\ RQ3) -- defense-in-depth is coverage, not redundancy.

\textbf{Limitations.} \label{sec:limitations} Each traces to a measured result, not an abstract concern. \emph{Live deployment}: the CARLA synthesizer defect (Section~\ref{sec:carla}) means every prior live evaluation never exposed ego-asserted attacks to B3, invalidating prior interpretation, not raw numbers; \texttt{sybil\_attack}/\texttt{semantic\_manipulation} remain B3-invisible post-fix (raw snake-case tags, unlike training distribution); CARLA's traffic manager is unseeded, so some scenario outcomes vary run-to-run. \emph{Evaluation coverage}: the adaptive-attack result (21.6\% ASR) is against the prior, not final, checkpoint; \textbf{so is the SUMO deployment replay (Fig.~\ref{fig_sumo_stage}) -- verified this pass (\texttt{DEPLOYMENT\_AUDIT.md}) by comparing file timestamps against the final checkpoint's; live CARLA (Table~\ref{tab:carla}) was identified as having the same gap and has since been rerun against the final checkpoint in this pass, resolving it for CARLA specifically, though as a single run rather than the two-run reproducibility comparison the prior checkpoint had; that rerun also surfaced a traced-but-unfixed pattern (three benign hazard-broadcast scenarios reaching 40/40 Reject, Section~\ref{sec:carla}), attributable to correctly-labeled but low-confidence B3 output on hazard-language text combined with B1's borderline validation score, not to a fusion or classification defect}; CP is zero-contribution due to a data-generation gap, not an algorithmic one. \emph{Benchmark/checkpoint}: a properly ensembled-fit calibration temperature improves ECE but degrades decision quality (F1 0.860$\to$0.817) via a floor/temperature coupling not yet jointly resolved (\texttt{CALIBRATION\_FIX\_REPORT.md}).

\textbf{Safety.} The question is not detection rate but whether a wrong decision reaches an unsafe maneuver with no sensor backstop. Post-fix, \texttt{authority\_override}/\texttt{false\_hazard\_clearance} are resolved; \texttt{sybil\_attack}/\texttt{semantic\_manipulation} remain a live, disclosed risk (MBD provides a partial backstop).

\textbf{Future work.} Render ego events as natural language (closing the B3 gap); seed CARLA for reproducibility; investigate the three benign CARLA scenarios' 40/40 Reject pattern surfaced by this pass's final-checkpoint rerun; rerun the SUMO deployment replay and the adaptive attack against the final checkpoint (CARLA's rerun is now done); populate CP's event field; batch B3 to close the throughput gap.

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================
This paper's contribution is an architecture, not a benchmark score: trust is decomposed into four questions -- sender, behavior, meaning, and the safest decision given all three -- answered by layers structurally blind to each other's evidence and combined through a Trust Decision Engine provably unable to relax a decision crypto/behavioral evidence alone would make more severe. Every experiment supports this design: independent per-layer contribution at zero cross-scope cost (RQ1), genuine held-out generalization (RQ2), coverage extending to real kinematic attacks (RQ3), and correct operation, with a disclosed throughput caveat, on live deployment surfaces (RQ4).

Deploying the unmodified pipeline against live CARLA surfaced and let us fix a synthesizer defect that had silently hidden attack content from the semantic layer -- evidence that claimed properties must be tested against live deployment, not only offline benchmarks. What remains open (RQ5) is stated plainly in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, not smoothed over; we treat this disclosure as a contribution in its own right.

Future work is two scoped extensions, not a redesign: rendering raw event tags as natural language to close B3's one coverage gap, and batching B3's forward pass to close the measured throughput gap.

\section{Proof Sketches for P1 and P3}
\label{app:proofs}
Full derivations are in \texttt{THEORETICAL\_ANALYSIS.md}; these are compact proof sketches for the two load-bearing properties, derived directly from \texttt{decision\_engine.py}'s control flow, not asserted informally.

\textbf{P1 (crypto rejection cannot be overridden).} Let $\ell_1$ be the level B1(+MBD+CP) alone would produce and $\ell$ the engine's final level, with $\mathrm{rank}(\text{ACCEPT}){=}0{<}\mathrm{rank}(\text{CAUTION}){=}1{<}\mathrm{rank}(\text{REJECT}){=}2$. If B1 is fatal, the engine returns REJECT before B3 is consulted, so $\mathrm{rank}(\ell){=}2\ge\mathrm{rank}(\ell_1)$ trivially. Otherwise the engine forms $\ell_{\text{ceil}}{=}\max_{\mathrm{rank}}(\ell_1,\ell_{\text{fused}})$, then applies two policy floors, each itself a rank-maximum against $\ell_{\text{ceil}}$ (HIGH semantic risk $\to\max_{\mathrm{rank}}(\ell_{\text{ceil}},\text{REJECT})$; MEDIUM/LOW $\to\max_{\mathrm{rank}}(\ell_{\text{ceil}},\text{CAUTION})$). A composition of rank-maximum operations against a value $\ge\ell_1$ stays $\ge\ell_1$, so $\mathrm{rank}(\ell)\ge\mathrm{rank}(\ell_1)$ always. $\blacksquare$ B3 can therefore only push the decision toward \emph{more} severity.

\textbf{P3 (fusion is provably conservative).} $\mathrm{rank}(\ell)\ge\max(\mathrm{rank}(\ell_1),\mathrm{rank}(\ell_{\text{fused}}))$ for every input, by the same construction: $\ell_{\text{ceil}}$ is already the rank-maximum of both operands, and the floors are rank-maximum against $\ell_{\text{ceil}}$, hence against both by transitivity. $\blacksquare$ No input can combine clean crypto evidence with ambiguous B3 evidence into a \emph{less} cautious decision than either alone would justify -- the guarantee is three explicit $\max$-operations in \texttt{decide()}, not an abstract property of Yager's rule.
