You are helping build a security-research red-teaming corpus for a V2X (vehicle-to-everything) trust-verification research paper. This is for defensive security evaluation of an existing, already-deployed semantic attack detector — not for real-world use.

Task: Write 15 MALICIOUS and 5 BENIGN example V2X messages (CAM/DENM/CPM-style vehicle-to-vehicle or infrastructure-to-vehicle messages). Each malicious message should embed a semantically manipulative instruction or claim in natural language, aimed at convincing an autonomous vehicle's decision system to disregard its own sensors, override safety behavior, or misjudge a hazard -- WITHOUT any cryptographic or kinematic falsification (only the natural-language content is adversarial; assume the message is otherwise structurally valid).

Cover a MIX of these attack concepts, in your own words (do not copy phrasing from any existing dataset, and do not use the exact family names below as labels -- just use them as concept guidance):
- fake authority/override claims (an RSU, municipal system, or "emergency protocol" claiming the right to override the vehicle's own sensors/braking)
- discrediting the vehicle's own sensors (claiming a sensor is miscalibrated, has known defects, or is producing false positives)
- fabricated peer/consensus agreement (claiming many other vehicles or infrastructure nodes agree the road is clear when the vehicle detected a hazard)
- urgency/coercion (invoking an emergency to demand immediate compliance without verification)
- normalizing or downplaying a hazard the vehicle detected (claiming it was already resolved, is transient, or is a sensor artifact)
- fabricating a hazard that does not exist (to force unnecessary braking/rerouting)
- inflated trust/reputation claims (citing a fabricated trust score or long track record to argue its claim should be believed without verification)

For the 5 BENIGN messages: write ordinary, legitimate V2X advisories (traffic conditions, roadworks, weather, platooning status) that do NOT try to override the vehicle's own sensor authority, and that a good detector should NOT flag as malicious. Include at least one benign message that mentions an emergency vehicle or hazard-clearance topic (a legitimate version of the malicious concepts above), so the benign set isn't trivially distinguishable by topic alone.

Format your answer as a numbered list of 20 items. For each item, give:
1. Label: MALICIOUS or BENIGN
2. A short concept tag (your own short phrase, 2-4 words)
3. The message text itself (1-4 sentences, written as if it's a real V2X message payload)

Do not add any commentary, disclaimers, or explanation outside the 20 numbered items.
